Laser Wall Offset Calibration

Mounting tolerances can slightly change the true angle of the lasers, which is why we must calibrate them for every machine individually and per laser. Higher offsets might be better for bigger parts to get larger chunk sizes working, though it results in a more defocused beam.

Uncalibrated No default offsets are shipped for safety reasons. Please calibrate the 5 fixed Z standoff heights below.

Calibration Steps

Download 10mm Cube STL
1

Print & Remove 10mm Cube

Print the 10x10x10 mm cube. Once done, remove the cube from the bed! Do not home the printer while the cube is on the bed.

2

Home Printer (Empty Bed)

Use your printer's menu to home all axes while the bed is completely empty. This ensures the printer finds its zero safely.

3

Place Cube Under Nozzle

Move the toolhead up so you can safely place the 10mm cube back onto the bed, directly under the nozzle.

4

Find Z Baseline

Place a piece of paper on the cube. Use the printer's menu to slowly lower the nozzle until you feel light friction on the paper. Remove the paper. This is your Z baseline height.

5

Align Nozzle to Cube Edge

Move the toolhead using the printer menu until the nozzle tip is perfectly flush with the outer vertical edge of the cube. This position is your X baseline.

6

Select Laser & Height

Decide which laser to calibrate first: Laser 1 (Right) or Laser 2 (Left). Choose a Z standoff height from the table (e.g., Level 2 at 10mm).

7

Move Z Up & Turn on Laser

Using the printer's menu, move the Z axis up by your chosen standoff height (e.g., move up 10mm). Then, run your printer's laser test macro to turn on the laser at a safe, low power.

8

Find the X Offset

Move the toolhead sideways (along the X axis) until the laser spot hits the vertical side wall of the cube. You need to align it so that the exact center of the laser spot rests perfectly on the center of the top layer on that side wall, not the absolute top edge of the part. Note down the distance you moved from your X baseline.

10mm Cube Wall
Correct: The exact center of the laser spot perfectly aligns with the center of the top layer (the middle of the layer's thickness).
9

Save Your Values

Enter the X distance into the table. Repeat this entire process for the other laser and for all the standoff heights you want to calibrate. Click Save to Browser and then Hard-Save Config (JSON).
